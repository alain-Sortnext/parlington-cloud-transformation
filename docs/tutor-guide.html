Programme Overview

Everything a tutor needs to understand the simulation before running a candidate through it

What this simulation is

Parlington Ltd is a fictional FCA/PRA-regulated UK asset manager (£4.2bn AUM) running a 9-phase AWS cloud transformation. The candidate plays the Senior AWS Solutions Architect. The simulation is built around a real broken codebase on GitHub, four real data files with intentional quality issues, and a cast of four stakeholder personas with genuinely competing priorities.

The simulation is hard by design. It is calibrated to the real standard of a Senior AWS SA hiring bar at UK financial services firms and Big Four consulting practices. A candidate who completes all 9 phases with strong submissions has genuinely earned the evidence they need for interviews at KPMG, Lloyds Banking Group, Goldman Sachs Engineering, and AWS Professional Services.

The business scenario in plain English

The situation a candidate walks into

  • Parlington is 14 months overdue on FCA Operational Resilience rules (PS21/3). The FCA has written twice. The fine could be up to £8.7M.
  • The previous architect (Craig Whitfield) quit two weeks ago. His handover notes are incomplete and only cover Stages 1–3.
  • The current infrastructure costs £3.8M/year. The board wants to get it to £1.9M/year. The transformation budget is £2.4M.
  • 120 servers across 2 data centres, 42 applications, 17 of them with zero disaster recovery.
  • A single legacy AWS account with 3 public S3 buckets, no GuardDuty, no CloudTrail, no SCPs.
  • The architecture review board meets at Week 36. The data centre must be fully exited by Week 76.

Wrong-Direction Traps

Three deliberate traps built into the data. A strong candidate avoids all three. A weak candidate falls into at least one.

🪤 Trap 1 — Windows Server is the Cost Driver (Phase 2)

The misleading signal: When a candidate looks at server-estate.csv and calculates average cost by OS, Windows Server 2016 appears expensive at ~£1,760/month average. Many candidates immediately conclude "Windows is the cost driver — migrate Windows first."

Why it is wrong: Windows servers exist across ALL tiers (web, app, database, monitoring) at very different price points. The average is pulled up by Windows database servers. The correct analysis is by CATEGORY (tier), not by OS.

The correct answer: The DATABASE TIER is the cost driver at ~55% of total weekly spend, regardless of operating system. This is confirmed by cross-referencing operational-report.csv (db_tier_cost_gbp column).

How to detect a candidate has fallen into the trap: Their report says "Windows Server 2016 is the primary cost driver" or "prioritise Windows migration for cost reduction."

How to detect a candidate avoided the trap: Their report explicitly says "The cost driver is the database TIER, not the Windows OS" and cites ~55% of spend from the operational report.

🪤 Trap 2 — Rehost is the Biggest Migration Workstream (Phase 2)

The misleading signal: The application portfolio shows 10 apps use Rehost strategy — the highest count of any single strategy. A candidate might conclude "Rehost is the primary workstream."

Why it is wrong: Rehost apps have low server counts (avg ~2 servers each = ~21 servers total). Replatform apps are fewer in number (14–16 apps) but have much higher server counts (avg ~3.5 each = ~52 servers total). By server volume — which drives infrastructure cost and migration complexity — Replatform dominates.

The correct answer: Replatform dominates by server count (52 vs 21) even though Rehost leads by app count (10 vs 14–16). Migration effort and cost reduction is driven by server volume, not app count.

How to detect the trap: Candidate says "we have more Rehost apps so that is our biggest workstream."

🪤 Trap 3 — Week 6 Cost Reduction Looks Like a Win (Phase 2)

The misleading signal: Week 6 shows total_infra_cost_gbp of ~£50k vs a normal average of ~£57k. A candidate might flag this as "early cost optimisation signs."

Why it is wrong: The notes column for Week 6 explicitly states: "AWS POC invoice not included due to billing cycle timing. NOT an optimisation." The lower cost is a billing delay artefact, not a genuine reduction.

The correct answer: Exclude Week 6 from trend analysis. Flag it as a data quality issue, not a cost reduction.

How to detect the trap: Candidate highlights Week 6 as positive cost movement or includes it in trend analysis without flagging the anomaly.

Stakeholder Conflicts

Three deliberate conflicts. A strong candidate resolves each with data, not politics.

⚔️ Conflict 1 — Priya vs Marcus on DR Timing (Phases 4 and 8)

Priya's position: DR must start now. We are in FCA breach. Four P1 Critical applications have zero DR. The FCA impact tolerance for IBS-001 is 4 hours and we already breached it.

Marcus's position: DR is expensive. Defer it to Phase 8 to protect programme budget. The FCA has not formally enforced yet.

The data that resolves it: Operational report shows 5 P1 incidents in 12 weeks, all on no-DR applications. Week 12 dual incident (CRP + OMS) lasted 6.7 hours — already exceeding the 4-hour FCA IBS-001 impact tolerance. This makes Marcus's deferral position indefensible to the FCA.

The correct recommendation: Hybrid. DR DESIGN completed in Phase 4 (gives Priya regulatory evidence she can show the FCA now). Full implementation in Phase 8 (gives Marcus the cost phasing he needs). This is the only answer that satisfies both without compromising either.

What a weak candidate does: Picks a side. Either "defer to Phase 8 because of budget" (ignores Priya's regulatory data) or "implement all DR now" (ignores Marcus's legitimate budget concern).

⚔️ Conflict 2 — Rachel vs Marcus on the Data OU (Phase 3)

Rachel's position (Data Analytics Lead): The data platform needs a dedicated Data OU with its own AWS account. Lake Formation governance requires account-level isolation for fine-grained column-level access control.

Marcus's position: A Data OU adds unnecessary complexity. Put it in the Workloads OU.

The correct answer: Rachel is architecturally correct. Lake Formation fine-grained access control (column-level permissions) works best with account-level isolation. Putting the data platform in the Workloads OU means sharing IAM permission boundaries with production application accounts — this creates data governance risk for GDPR-regulated data. The Data OU is the right call.

Justification to give Marcus: The Data OU adds one more account but prevents the risk of cross-account data access that would require a full Lake Formation re-architecture later. One-time setup cost vs ongoing governance complexity.

⚔️ Conflict 3 — Rachel vs David on Platform Control (Phase 6)

Rachel's position (Dev Lead): Developers should have self-service EKS deployment. Give teams control over their namespaces.

David's position (Head of Infra): Central infrastructure team must control the cluster. Developers cannot be given kubectl access to production.

The correct answer: Namespace-level RBAC with GitOps enforcement. Developers own their namespaces (trading-prod, payments-prod) — they can deploy, scale, and manage their services. Infrastructure owns the cluster — node groups, NetworkPolicies, cluster add-ons, RBAC rules. ArgoCD AppProjects enforce which repositories can deploy to which namespaces. This gives Rachel self-service within boundaries and gives David cluster governance.

What a weak candidate does: Picks a side without proposing the architectural pattern that satisfies both.

Phase 2 — Discovery Assessment

Weeks 1–4 · Pass score: 65% · Phase type: discovery

What this phase is for

Phase 2 is the most important analytical phase of the simulation. It tests whether a candidate can handle messy real-world data, avoid the wrong-direction trap, cross-reference multiple files, and produce a board-ready report. Most candidates can write a report — very few can correctly identify the database tier as the cost driver AND explain why the Windows OS metric was misleading.

Step-by-step: how to complete Phase 2

1
Document data quality issues BEFORE calculating anything

Open all three CSV files and write down every quality issue you find before touching a calculator or spreadsheet formula. This forces you to know what data you can trust. The grading checks require specific data quality issues to be named. Generic statements like "the data had some issues" score zero.

Complete list of data quality issues to document

server-estate.csv: SRV-047 duplicate (2 rows for same server_id); 8 blank monthly_cost_gbp values; 3 date formats in last_patched column (YYYY-MM-DD, DD/MM/YYYY, DD-MM-YYYY); 6 variants of "NO DR" in dr_status column; 15 OS naming variants; some age values in "N months" format not years.

application-portfolio.csv: APP-019 duplicate; 3 different dependency delimiters (pipe |, comma, semicolon ;); mixed complexity formats (7/10, High, Medium-High); 6 blank annual_licence_cost_gbp values; inconsistent capitalisation.

operational-report.csv: Week 3 appears twice (use "Week 3 revised"); Week 6 billing anomaly (notes column explains); Week 7 blank db_tier_cost_gbp; Week 9 shows "TBC" for p1_incidents; patch_compliance_pct uses both decimal (0.71) and percentage (71%) formats.

2
State your assumptions explicitly — which data did you trust?

Before any analysis, write a trust statement. Example: "I excluded the 8 servers with blank cost data from cost analysis. I used the application-portfolio.csv as the authoritative source for DR status (not server-estate.csv, which has 6 inconsistent DR status variants). I excluded Week 6 from operational trend analysis due to the billing delay noted in the notes column."

3
Calculate the server estate summary

Use a spreadsheet or Python script. Here are the exact steps:

  1. Remove the SRV-047 duplicate row (keep the PAR-DB-047 hostname row)
  2. Filter is_eol = "True" → count → 25 EOL servers
  3. Normalise dr_status to find all no-DR variants → count → 47 servers with no DR
  4. Filter category = "Database" → count → 29 database servers
  5. Sum monthly_cost_gbp excluding blanks → ~£239,000/month total
  6. Average monthly_cost_gbp where category = Database → £4,822/server/month
  7. Average where OS contains "Windows" → £1,760/server/month
4
Calculate the application portfolio summary
  1. Remove the APP-019 duplicate row
  2. Normalise dependency delimiters — convert all | and ; to comma, then count values
  3. Normalise complexity formats — map "7/10"→7, "High"→8, "Medium-High"→7, "Medium"→5 etc.
  4. Count apps with complexity ≥7 → 14–15 high complexity apps
  5. Filter criticality = P1 AND dr_status = NO DR variants → 4 apps: APP-001 PEMIS, APP-002 OMS, APP-003 CRP, APP-004 RRG
  6. Count apps with dependency_count >3 → 6 apps
  7. Check: all 6 high-dep apps also score ≥7 complexity → 100% correlation
5
Run the cost driver analysis — cross-reference both files

This is the most important step. Do NOT just look at server-estate.csv alone.

  1. Open operational-report.csv
  2. Exclude Week 6 (billing anomaly) and Week 7 (blank DB cost)
  3. For remaining weeks: sum db_tier_cost_gbp and sum total_infra_cost_gbp
  4. Divide: SUM(db_tier) ÷ SUM(total) = ~54.9% ≈ 55%
  5. Now compare to server-estate.csv: DB avg £4,822 vs Windows avg £1,760
  6. Conclusion: The database TIER drives cost (55% of spend), not Windows OS.
  7. Write explicitly: "The surface metric (Windows avg cost) is misleading because Windows servers span multiple tiers. The correct analysis by tier shows the database category accounts for approximately 55% of total spend."
6
Document the critical open questions

These must be in your report. They block subsequent phases if unanswered:

  • OQ-001: Mystery IP range 10.200.0.0/16 — origin unknown. Blocks VPC CIDR design. Ben Hartley needs to investigate.
  • OQ-002: Direct Connect not ordered. 8–12 week lead time. This is already on the programme critical path. Action: order immediately.
  • OQ-004: Unclassified S3 bucket in legacy account. Potential UK GDPR Article 33 data breach notification. DPO assessment required within 72 hours of awareness.
7
Create the current-state architecture diagram in Draw.io

Open app.diagrams.net. Add AWS shape library (+ button → search AWS). Create two data centre zones (Equinix LD4 and Canary Wharf colo). Within each zone, create tier swim lanes (Web/App, Database, Middleware, Batch/ETL, Monitoring). Add the AWS POC account as a separate zone on the right. Mark the 4 P1 Critical no-DR apps with red borders. Show network connectivity (10Gbps MPLS between DCs, internet to AWS). Commit both the .drawio file and a PNG export to /architecture/current-state/ in your GitHub fork.

8
Write the Discovery Assessment Report

Structure: (1) Data Quality Assessment, (2) Server Estate Summary table, (3) Application Portfolio Summary table, (4) Cost Driver Analysis with cross-reference, (5) DR Gap Assessment with named P1 apps, (6) Critical Open Questions table, (7) Prioritised Recommendations. Each recommendation must cite a specific figure from the data.

✅ Strong submission

  • Documents specific data quality issues (names SRV-047 duplicate, 8 blank costs, 6 DR status variants)
  • States trust assumptions explicitly before analysis
  • Cites DB tier as 49–58% of spend from operational report
  • Explicitly names the misleading Windows metric and explains why it is wrong
  • Names all 4 P1 Critical no-DR apps (PEMIS, OMS, CRP, RRG)
  • Flags all 3 critical open questions
  • 100% correlation finding for high-dep = high-complexity

❌ Weak submission

  • "The data had quality issues" — no specifics
  • Concludes Windows is the cost driver without cross-referencing operational report
  • Calculates EOL count as 25 but doesn't flag it's NOT the cost driver
  • Says "17 apps have no DR" without naming which P1 Criticals are at risk
  • Skips the open questions entirely
  • Uses server-estate.csv DR column as authoritative (it is not)
  • Includes Week 6 in cost trend analysis

Phase 3 — Landing Zone, Architecture & Governance

Weeks 5–8 · Pass score: 70% · Phase type: work

Step-by-step: how to complete Phase 3

1
Complete ADR-001 — the rationale section is blank

The draft ADR-001 in the repo has three options: (1) Single account with tagging, (2) Manual multi-account, (3) AWS Control Tower. Choose Option 3. Rationale: automated guardrails prevent drift; account vending via Account Factory reduces provisioning from weeks to hours; native Security Hub + CloudTrail integration satisfies FCA SS2/21; SCPs apply at OU level with inheritance. Commit to /docs/adrs/ADR-001-multi-account-structure.md.
